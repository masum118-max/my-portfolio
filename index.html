    About

    Academic Biography

    I completed my B.Sc. in Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology (CUET) in 2024 and am currently pursuing my M.Sc. in the same department. My undergraduate training gave me a foundation in circuits, digital systems, and power engineering, which I continue to build on through graduate study.

    Since 2024 my research has moved steadily toward computation. I have applied machine learning and deep learning to energy problems, including electric vehicle charging load forecasting and electricity usage and billing trends, and I have published conference papers on these and related topics. In parallel, I study quantum mechanics and quantum computing: I simulate quantum circuits and algorithms in Qiskit and have worked on a Schrödinger-equation-based approach to detecting defects in dielectric and meta-materials, which connects my interest in quantum theory with photonics and materials.

    I also work as an Adjunct Lecturer in the Department of Computer Science & Engineering at International Islamic University Chittagong (IIUC), where I teach Microprocessors and Digital Logic Design, prepare lecture materials and assessments, and supervise student hardware and software projects. Teaching keeps my fundamentals sharp and has shaped how I explain technical ideas.

    My long-term goal is to pursue advanced research in quantum information science, with a focus on quantum computing and quantum machine learning, and to contribute to the international scientific community. I intend to build on my engineering and computational background and to develop the theoretical depth that research in this field requires.

    Research Interests

    Research Areas

    My research direction runs from electrical engineering through data-driven energy modelling toward quantum information science, with quantum computing and quantum machine learning as the long-term focus.

    Quantum Computing and Quantum Information Science

    I am interested in how quantum states, entanglement, and quantum algorithms process information, and in the physics that underpins them, including quantum mechanics and its links to photonics. My current work is computational: simulating Bell states, teleportation, superdense coding, and the Deutsch–Jozsa algorithm in Qiskit. I aim to move toward algorithm design and the theory of quantum information.

    Quantum Cryptography and Quantum Cybersecurity

    I want to study how quantum mechanics enables secure communication, for example quantum key distribution, and how quantum computing changes the threat model for classical cryptography. This is an area I am learning and plan to examine through simulation and the literature.

    Quantum Machine Learning

    I am exploring hybrid classical–quantum learning, starting with knowledge distillation from classical neural network teachers into quantum student models. The central question for me is where quantum models can be competitive under realistic constraints on qubits and circuit depth.

    Artificial Intelligence and Deep Learning

    I use machine learning and deep learning, including gradient-boosted trees, support vector machines, CNNs, and LSTMs, to model real-world data. I am interested in building models that are accurate, carefully evaluated, and reproducible.

    Smart Energy Systems

    Using data-driven methods to understand and plan electricity demand is a natural extension of my electrical engineering training. My work includes analysis of electricity usage and billing trends in Chittagong and its implications for sustainable energy planning.

    Electric Vehicle Charging Load Forecasting

    Accurate forecasts of charging-station load support grid planning and operation as electric vehicles become more common. I have applied machine learning to this problem and plan to extend it with deep sequence models and richer feature sets.

    Renewable Energy Technologies

    I am interested in the integration of renewable sources into power systems, particularly how forecasting and intelligent control can help manage variability. This is a broader interest that I intend to connect with my forecasting work.

    Bell States and Entanglement: A Short Primer

    How two gates produce a maximally entangled pair of qubits, and why measuring them reveals correlations that no classical model can reproduce on its own.

    Quantum ComputingEntanglement

    Read note

    A Bell state is a two-qubit state in which the qubits are maximally entangled. The simplest to prepare is (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2. Starting from |00⟩, a Hadamard gate on the first qubit creates an equal superposition, and a CNOT gate controlled by that qubit flips the second qubit in the |1⟩ branch. The result cannot be written as a product of two single-qubit states.

    What entanglement means operationally

    If both qubits of this state are measured in the computational basis, each outcome on its own is random, 0 or 1 with equal probability, yet the two outcomes always agree. The information is in the correlation rather than in either qubit separately. Entanglement of this kind is the resource behind protocols such as quantum teleportation and superdense coding.

    Why it matters beyond the circuit

    Bell's theorem shows that the correlations of such states, measured in suitably chosen bases, violate inequalities that any local hidden-variable theory must satisfy. This is what makes entanglement a genuinely non-classical resource, and it is why Bell-state preparation is a standard first experiment when learning to work with quantum circuits in a simulator such as Qiskit.
